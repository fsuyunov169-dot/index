Step 1

What is the Past Continuous?

We use it for an action that was in progress at a specific moment in the past. It was already happening, and it was not finished yet.

started
⏰
finished
At 8 pm yesterday, I was doing my homework.Not “I did my homework”. The focus is on the action in progress.
Step 2

How do we make it?

I / he / she / it + was + verb-ing
you / we / they + were + verb-ing

Try the verb machine. Pick a subject and a verb.

Step 3

Positive, negative and questions

TypeStructureExample
Positivesubject + was/were + verb-ingShe was reading.
Negativesubject + wasn’t/weren’t + verb-ingThey weren’t playing.
Yes/No questionWas/Were + subject + verb-ing?Was he sleeping?

Short answers

Were you studying? — Yes, I was. / No, I wasn’t.
Was she cooking? — Yes, she was. / No, she wasn’t.
Were they running? — Yes, they were. / No, they weren’t.
Step 4

Three ways to use it

1. A moment in the past

At 9 pm last night, we were watching a film.

Time expressions: at 5 o’clock yesterday, at that moment, this time last week, all day.

2. Interrupted action

I was cooking when the phone rang.

Long action → Past Continuous. Short event → Past Simple.

3. Two actions together

While I was reading, he was sleeping.

Use while to join two actions in progress at the same time.

Step 5

Past Continuous or Past Simple?

Past ContinuousPast Simple
Meaningaction in progressfinished action or event
ExampleI was eating at 7.I ate dinner at 7.
Lengthlonger, backgroundshort, main event
Key wordwhilewhen (+ short event)
I was walking home when it started to rain.Walking = background. Started = interruption.
It started to rain while I was walking home.Same meaning. Only the order changes.
Step 6

Spelling the -ing form

RuleExamples
Most verbs: add -ingplay → playing, read → reading
Ends in -e: drop the emake → making, write → writing
Short vowel + consonant: double itrun → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming
Ends in -ie: change to -ylie → lying, die → dying
Ends in -y: just add -ingstudy → studying, fly → flying
Step 7

Common mistakes

✗ She were singing. → ✓ She was singing.
✗ They was waiting. → ✓ They were waiting.
✗ I was runing. → ✓ I was running.
✗ Did you studying? → ✓ Were you studying?
✗ I was knowing the answer. → ✓ I knew the answer.State verbs (know, like, love, want, believe) are not normally used in continuous tenses.
For teachers

45-minute lesson plan

  • 5 min · Warm-up. Ask three students: “What were you doing at 7 pm yesterday?”
  • 10 min · Present. Show the Learn tab on the board. Use the timeline and the verb machine.
  • 10 min · Quiz. Whole class. Students vote for A, B or C before you reveal the answer.
  • 10 min · Build It. Pairs: one student clicks, the other checks.
  • 8 min · Memory. Two teams take turns.
  • 2 min · Homework. Write five sentences about what you were doing yesterday at different times.

Activities

More classroom ideas

  • The Alibi. “A cake disappeared at 4 pm.” Pairs invent alibis. The class asks: “What were you doing at 4?”
  • Mime. A student acts out an action, the class guesses: “You were swimming!”
  • Story chain. “While I was cooking, …” Each student adds one sentence.
  • Picture detective. Show a busy street photo for 30 seconds. Students write what people were doing.
  • Interrupted! Write the first half of a sentence on the board. Students add a Past Simple interruption: “I was sleeping when…”
